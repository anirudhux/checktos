# Jev — Terms of Use analysis

Jev's terms, published under TypeSafe AI, Inc. and titled "Terms of Use," govern the typesafe.ai website. This analysis explains what the document says for a general reader. One thing sets the frame for everything below: this page covers the marketing website only, and it states that any use of the actual product is governed by a separate agreement that is not on this page. So this reading is partial and does not describe the product.

## How hard it is to read

Moderate. The document is shorter than a full product agreement and boilerplate in form, with three all-caps blocks. The harder problem is not the reading level: the terms a product user would need are not on this page.

## Product

This page says almost nothing about a product. It states that "the Site provides information about TypeSafe and its products and services" (Section 1), and that any real use is elsewhere: "If you enter into a separate agreement with TypeSafe for the use of any TypeSafe products or services... the terms of that separate agreement will govern" (Preamble). The license you get here is only to browse: "a limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable, non-sublicensable, revocable license to access and use the Site" (Section 3a).

## Usage

Standard browsing restrictions apply: no illegal use, no infringing or modifying the site, no reverse engineering, and no scraping or automated access (Section 3b). The site "is intended for visitors located within the United States" (Section 13g). There are no accounts or data-input terms here, because this is an informational site.

## Feedback and improvement

The one clause with real weight is about feedback. Anything you send is non-confidential, and you grant TypeSafe "an unrestricted, perpetual, irrevocable, non-exclusive, fully paid-up, royalty-free, transferable, sublicensable right to exploit the Feedback in any manner and for any purpose" (Section 3c). They also tell you plainly: "Do not submit any information or other materials that you consider confidential or proprietary through the Site." There is no model-training clause here, because this page covers the website rather than the product.

## Legal ramifications

Disputes go to individual arbitration through JAMS, and you waive class actions and a jury trial (Sections 12a, 12h). You can opt out, "but only by sending a letter" to a San Francisco address within 30 days (Section 12c), which is a higher bar than an emailed opt-out. Liability is capped: "THE AGGREGATE LIABILITY OF THE TYPESAFE ENTITIES TO YOU FOR ALL CLAIMS... IS LIMITED TO $100 USD" (Section 11b). You indemnify TypeSafe one way (Section 9). TypeSafe "reserve[s] the right to change these Terms... at any time" and binds you to the latest version on each use (Section 6). Governing law is Delaware.
